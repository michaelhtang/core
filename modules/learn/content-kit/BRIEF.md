## The rule for numbers (the most important rule)
- **Pay, deductions, job wages, hours, rent, bills, anything the game itself computes:** take it from the game's own module (`modules/jobs/payroll.js`, `modules/jobs/catalogue.js`, `modules/housing`, `modules/bills`, ...) so a student's own screen agrees with the lesson. - **Anything in neither:** write the idea with NO number, and list it in your REVIEW file as "written without a number". Never use a figure from your own memory, however sure you are. No outside salary figures, no statistics, no dates of laws.
- Made-up people with made-up small amounts in a word problem are fine ("Dev spends $12.00 a week on coffee"): they are arithmetic, not facts. Keep them plausible for a teenager or a young adult in Edmonton, in Canadian dollars.

## Voice and reading level
- Grade 8 to 9 reading level. Short sentences. One idea per card, at most 320 characters. Plain words; define a money word the first time on a card and add it to the question's `terms` where it helps.
- Speak to the student as "you". Calm and direct. No hype, no exclamation marks, no jokes at anyone's expense, no moralising about what people should want.
- Canadian spelling (cheque, colour, labour). Money as `$1,234.56`. Dates and rules are Alberta's and Canada's.
- People in examples: first names only, a mix a Grade 10 class in Edmonton would recognise as their own city, never a real person, never a name that reads as a joke. No student is ever named. No brand or company names (the game's own fictional employers from `modules/jobs/catalogue.js` are fine).
- Nothing about a student's own family, home, income or body. Where Mr. Tang's lesson asks students about their own life, teach the idea through a made-up person instead. The classroom activities (games, articles, quizzes from other publishers) are NOT to be reproduced or described step by step: teach the idea underneath them in your own words.
- Attribution: if a line needs to say who teaches the course, it is "Mr. Tang". Never mention any tool or AI.

## Questions
- Every question has exactly one right answer that a careful student can reach from the cards. No opinion questions, no "which would you choose", no "all of the above" or "none of the above", no trick wording, no double negatives.
- `choice`: 3 or 4 options, every option has a `why` that teaches (why it is right, or what mistake leads to it). Wrong options are real mistakes a student makes, not filler. Options of similar length (the check enforces this). Vary where the right option sits (the check enforces this too).
- `number`: the student types a number. Give `unit` (`$`, `%`, `hours`), `answer`, `tolerance`, `why`, `working` (the worked steps), `hint`, `miss` (shown on a wrong answer; guides without giving the answer), and `traps` for the common wrong answers where there is one.
- `point`: tap a bar or a point on a `bars` or `line` figure.
- Aim for at least 2 number questions and at least 1 question with a figure in every level where the topic has numbers. A level about ideas (values, habits) may have fewer; do not force arithmetic that teaches nothing.
- Questions go from recall, to using the idea on a new case, to a small multi-step problem. Reuse an idea from an earlier level of your unit in a later one at least once per level (spaced practice).
- `learn` on a level is two short lines saying what the student will be able to do.
- Where it fits, give the LAST level (or one other) a `go` button that sends the student into a building of the game to do the real thing (see how level 7 of the pay unit does it; building keys are in `docs/learn.md` and the modules).
- Stable ids: `<unit-id>-l<n>` and `<unit-id>-l<n>-q<n>`.
