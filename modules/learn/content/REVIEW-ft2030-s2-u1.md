## Definitions and rules in our words, for Mr. Tang to confirm

13. **Business concept:** "a short, clear description of a business idea" that answers four questions: product or service, customer, benefit, how the customer gets it (the outline's four elements, with "distribution" as the fourth part).
14. **Feature and benefit:** a feature describes the product; a benefit is "what the product does about the customer's problem". The outline's second wording ("a feature describes your product, a benefit describes your customer's problem") is our reading of the same idea.
15. **The person who uses is not always the person who pays** (level 1 card 2): from the outline's note that parents hold the money; our example (art kits, Grade 4) is made up.
16. **Distribution:** "how the product gets to the customer: where and when".
17. **Format:** two to four sentences, no bullet points, clear read out loud (outline Lesson 1, slide 11). The "after one listen, name the customer and the benefit" test is the outline's share-out rule.
18. **Feasibility:** three questions (market, reach, win), from the outline; "it needs evidence" is ours.
19. **Market:** "the right people, at the right time, with the right need and money" (our wording of the outline's market idea). The honest market must be bigger than the break-even point (outline Lesson 3, slide 5); if it is smaller, the plan "must change".
20. **Direct, indirect and doing nothing:** direct = same thing, same customer; indirect = wins the same dollar another way; doing nothing = buying nothing at all. The outline's own examples are replaced with made-up ones; the idea that non-consumption is usually the biggest competitor is NOT claimed (we say only that it is a competitor too).
21. **Four reasons to win:** cheaper, closer, better, different (outline Lesson 3, slide 7). "Better" and "Different" are named but not defined further; "Ours is good" is shown as not a reason.
22. **Competitive matrix:** a list of competitors side by side with a strength and a weakness for each (the FT10 wording); the outline's "minimum four rows" and "reason to win must be one of four words" appear only as a teaching pattern, not as a rule of the unit.
23. **Survey rules** (level 3): evidence against opinion, ask about the past, no leading questions with real alternatives, one thing per question, quick and mostly multiple choice with at most one open box, five things to learn. From the outline's four rules and five-question skeleton, in our own words and examples. Our rule that price should be asked as a choice of prices including "would not buy", because people say they would pay more than they do, comes from the outline's Lesson 4 slide 10 note for the Grade 12 group; it is taught to everyone here.
24. **Sample:** the class that answers is not the whole market, so ask a few outside people (level 3 card 6, and q9, q10). The outline's "three outside responses each" is not used.
