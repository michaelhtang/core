## The game

16. **The game's credit score is described from the code, as a simplified version.** Level 5 cards 1 to 6: 300 to 900; five factors with weights 35, 30, 15, 10 and 10 (210, 180, 90, 60 and 60 points); a thin file starts at 70% of the payment points and gains 10% per month with an on-time payment; a late mark takes 25% off and fades over 24 game months; the card-balance table (10% 100%, 30% 80%, 50% 50%, 75% 20%, 100% 0%, sliding between); full length points at 36 months; applications counted over 12 months. Source: `modules/credit/constants.js`, `score.js`, `docs/credit.md`. The code itself says the weights and curves are illustrative (the 35/30/15/10/10 split was copied from consumer-finance sites that say the bureaus do not publish theirs), so the lesson calls it "the game's own simplified version". If the game's constants change, `test/learn-content-ft10-s3-u1.test.js` fails.
17. **What the score changes for you in the game: among lenders, the card only** (it also unlocks an Outfitters achievement at 750 and counts on the leaderboards). The card application declines if average pay over the last 3 months is under $1,000 a month before deductions, if the score (when there is one) is under 560, or at 4 applications in 12 months; an approved limit is average pay x a share that rises from 30% at a score of 560 to 100% at 800, rounded down to $100 and kept between $500 and $2,500. Level 5 cards 5 and 6, q5 to q7. Housing's mortgage code uses only the card's minimum payment (for its debt ratio), and the stress loan does not read the score, so the lesson says nothing about a mortgage or the stress loan.
18. **The game's pay check is separate from the score.** The score has no pay factor, but the card's approval checks pay. Level 5 card 5, ft10-s3-u1-l5-q9. This is the same point as your "income is not in the score".
19. **The amount $2,150 in ft10-s3-u1-l5-q5 is deliberate.** At exactly $2,000 pay and a 680 score the exact answer is $1,300.00, but the game's floating-point arithmetic returns $1,200 (65% comes out as 0.6499999). A student's own limit could be off by $100 in an edge case like that; the question uses a pay figure that is not on a $100 boundary. You may want to know the game has this quirk.
20. **The town's price index does not matter here.** The credit constants (the $1,000, $500 and $2,500) are not scaled by the price index, and the unit uses no pay, wage or price from the game. The Credit Bureau screen shows the score on a dial, the five factors with points and a tip each, and (without a card) the "no credit history yet" message; the `go` button on level 5 opens that building (`credit`). The lesson does not ask the student to apply for the card.
