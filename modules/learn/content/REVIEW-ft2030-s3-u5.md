## Definitions and rules in our words, for Mr. Tang to confirm

8. **Company risk and market risk** (level 1, cards 2 and 4; terms on l1-q1, q2, q4, q6). Our names for what Lesson 25 slide 5 calls the risk that more companies fixes (blue) and the risk that only spreading across places and sectors fixes (orange). Please confirm you want those two words.

No tab fixes that"). Stated as an idea, no number.
10. **Size, style and the four sector groups** (level 2, cards 2 and 3). Size is only "large or small": no dollar line, since the outline gives none. Style: growth "sells at a high price because buyers expect fast growth", value "sells at a low price compared with what it earns" (the two meanings on Lesson 25 slide 4, reworded). The four sector group names are outcome 1.22's own list; the one-line meaning of each (banks and others whose business moves with interest rates; sells to everyday shoppers; builds and moves things; oil, mines, forests) is ours, made from the Lesson 14 company sort ("Consumer: merchandise", "Resources: oil and gas", "Industrial: transportation, technology", "Interest sensitive: banks"). 11. **Geography** (level 2, card 4): Canada, the United States, other countries (international) and emerging markets, as on Lesson 25 slide 4. "Emerging markets: countries whose markets are younger and growing fast" is our wording, not the outline's.
12. **"Companies in one country tend to rise and fall together"; "companies in one sector tend to fall together"** (level 2 cards 4 and 6; level 3 card 4; l2-q9, l3-q7). Taken from the Lesson 25 Q2 result and the huddle note ("things that fall together don't diversify each other", two bank-heavy funds). The wording "move almost like one" is ours.
13. **A fund in one clause, holdings and weight** (level 3, card 1): "one purchase that holds many companies at once", as the Lesson 14 index-fund note says ("one purchase that holds everything on a list"). A holding is one company in the fund; a weight is a share of the money as a percent. The three facts to read (holdings, largest sector and weight, share in each country) are Lesson 25 slide 6's three columns. The one place the unit says what an index fund is (level 5, card 6 and the term on l5-q6) is "a fund that holds every company on a list, such as every company in town", from the Lesson 14 note and the Exchange's note on its index fund ("every company in town in one fund"). The unit does not define a mutual fund or a fee; "Funds and fees" does. The terms "GIC" (a loan to a bank for a set time) and "steady part" on level 5 follow the wording of `ft10-s2-u4` level 2 card 5.
14. **Mixing funds** (level 3, card 5; l3-q5, q9): the share of a mix in one country is each fund's share there times its weight in your money, added up. This is the idea of the `=SUMPRODUCT` blend in the 30s' huddle, in words; no spreadsheet function is named. Level 3, card 6 repeats the outline's rule: write the source and the date beside every number copied from a fund's page.
15. **Blended return** (level 4, cards 1 to 3): the return of a mix is each part's weight times its return, added up; it leans toward the part with more money; it can never be above the best part. The see-saw picture is ours (the outline's picture is two jars). The wrong answer "the middle" is the outline's own predicted misconception.
16. **REIT, units, distributions** (level 4, card 4): "owns buildings that earn rent (apartments, warehouses, offices); you buy units like shares; pays most of its rent out as distributions, often every month." Follows Lesson 29 slide 4 (which lists apartments, malls, warehouses, offices and says most Canadian REITs pay every month); "malls" is left out, "often" is ours, and the unit does not say "Canadian". The outline's slide gives its risk as Medium ("the brief's table"); the unit gives no risk label.
17. **A REIT's return is its yield plus the price change; the yield is the yearly payout as a percent of the price** (level 4, card 5; l4-q9). The "yield plus price change" rule is the outline's (slide 4 and the F12 cell note); the one-clause meaning of yield is ours. l4-q9 (yield 4%, price falls 1%, return 3%) is made-up arithmetic. Adding the two is a simple approximation: it treats the price change as a percent of the starting price.
18. **The 6% a year for the REIT, and the 8%, 4%, 3% rates** (level 4, card 5 and q7; level 5, q8). 8% (stocks), 4% (bonds) and 3% (cash) are the outlines' own made-up rates (lines 154 to 157 and 496 of Lesson 29).
