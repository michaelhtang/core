## Definitions and rules in our words, for Mr. Tang to confirm

14. Sample: "the group of people who answered your survey". In-class sample: people who know the team. Outside sample: people who do not (level 1 card 1). The outline's "in-class" and "outside" are the class's own Form counts; ours is the idea only.
15. Sampling bias: "when the group that answered leans one way, so the result is not a fair picture of your real customers" (card 2). Friends being kind is the outline's reason (Lesson 5 slide 3); "so they say yes and name a high price" is our wording of the outline's notes (outside respondents give lower prices and blunter answers, slide 2 notes).
16. "Plan with the outside number" (card 4) follows the outline's "The strangers. Every time." (slide 3) but is softened: we add that a very small outside sample is thin.
17. Percentage points: "the difference between two percentages" (level 1 question 4 term).
18. The four options: keep (the data supports the idea as written), reprice (right product, wrong price), reposition (right product, wrong customer or moment), replace (the data says no). Wording follows Lesson 5 slide 4. "Keep needs evidence, not just a lack of bad news" follows the slide 4 notes.
19. A pivot "changes one part of the concept: the product, the customer, the benefit or how they get it" (card 6) follows the Lesson 5 slide 5 notes (a change to one variable in the concept statement).
20. Whole-dollar price: when payment comes only in $1 units, a price must be a whole number of dollars (level 2 card 1; Lesson 7 slides 2 and 3).
21. Payment system: "a set of rules that moves money from buyers to sellers" (card 2). Our definition; the outline calls the tickets scrip and a payment system (slide 5) without defining the term.
22. Liability: "something you owe, or may have to pay back" (term). A token not handed back is a liability "because the office still owes that value" (card 2; Lesson 7 slide 5, "a ticket is a liability until it's redeemed").
23. Rounding trade-off (level 2 card 3): down gives lower margin, more buyers and more items to break even; up gives higher margin, fewer buyers and fewer items to break even (Lesson 7 slide 4 table).
24. Money left = buyers x margin - fixed costs (level 2 card 6). Our name for "profit at this price before anything else". Buyers = people who pass x share who would buy: an assumption of our made-up example, not a rule in the outline.
25. Sources of funds (level 3 card 1): sales (what customers pay), fundraising (money given for a purpose), supplier credit (take goods now, pay later), a grant (money from a society, foundation or government that is not paid back). The four names follow the ENT2020 2.5 list (suppliers, fundraising, sales, societies, foundations, government). The outline does not teach these; the definitions are ours and "not paid back" is our wording of a grant.
26. Use of funds, purpose and disbursement (card 2): a named recipient, two sentences on why, and "who holds it, who checks the total and how it is paid out" (Lesson 8 slides 5, 6 and 9). The outline's own disbursement answer names Mr. Tang as holder; ours says "the office" in the question 4 option, a made-up school.
27. Net funds = total raised - total spent; only the net goes to the cause (card 3; Lesson 8 slide 8). The outline's five-day reporting window is not used.
28. Impact on school operations: "say the impact, then the fix" (card 4; Lesson 7 slide 10 notes, "they write the impact and stop"). How the purpose is communicated: a poster that names the cause (Lesson 8 slide 7).
29. Plan for unsold items: sell cheaper on the last day, give it away, take orders first, or make small batches and restock; each has a cost (card 5; Lesson 5 slide 10). The outline's note that taking orders cannot happen before approval is not used.
30. Purchase list: exact item and size, packs, price per pack and where the price came from; you buy whole packs (card 6; Lesson 9 slides 4 to 7). "Where the price came from" is the outline's "real unit cost from a real source".
